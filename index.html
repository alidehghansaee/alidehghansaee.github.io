About

Ali Dehghan Saee is a researcher in international economic law and climate governance, with an interdisciplinary background spanning law, economics, energy, and engineering. His research examines how legal systems respond to the growing incorporation of climate objectives into economic regulation.

His broader research programme investigates how economic openness can be reconciled with ecological constraint while preserving non-discrimination, regulatory diversity, institutional legitimacy, and fairness across jurisdictions with unequal capacities.

Research for a fairer and more sustainable global economy.

International Economic Law

WTO law, non-discrimination, environmental exceptions, and the interaction between trade disciplines and contemporary climate regulation.

Climate-Conditioned Economic Governance

Carbon Border Measures

04

Regulatory Pluralism & Climate Federalism

Research programme

Current Research

01

Climate-Conditioned Economic Governance

A theoretical account of how climate objectives are becoming embedded within the institutional architecture of economic governance.

02

Differentiation without Discrimination

Reconciling climate-related differentiation with international economic law through regulatory equivalence, capacity sensitivity, and procedural fairness.

03

Climate Federalism as Regulatory Pluralism

Examining Canadian climate federalism through minimum standards, equivalence, regulatory recognition, and substitution.

04

The Emerging Law of Carbon Borders

A comparative analysis of EU and UK carbon-border regimes and their contribution to a broader transnational legal architecture.
